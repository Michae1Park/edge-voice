Compound Stress Boundary

Three successive sustained-load experiments, each designed to correct what the previous one got wrong. The headline result changed as the investigation went deeper: what first looked like a clean multi-dimensional capacity ceiling turned out to be a rare, content-triggered VAD/TSM interaction — not a predictable resource limit.

Raspberry Pi 5 · 4 core Moonshine tiny-ko, single-threaded ORT single channel (rx) reliability disabled vad.min_silence_duration_ms=50 39 cells across 3 experiments

Method

Three variables, isolated

Rate: pitch-preserving TSM, capped at 2× — TSM's own audio quality degrades past that, which would confound "system can't keep up" with "audio became unintelligible."

Length: real recordings, then (experiment 3) a single recording cropped to different durations — see below on why this changed.

Density: silence-gap tiling, gaps always ≥2.8× vad.min_silence_duration_ms to avoid re-triggering an already-fixed VAD-threshold bug from an earlier investigation.

Objective pass/fail

A cell fails if segment capture rate drops below 95%, or pre_stt_latency exceeds 3× that segment's own decode time for 3+ consecutive segments (a plateau, not a one-off blip).

A third signal, merge events (added for experiments 2-3): a segment-start-to-segment-start gap >1.3× the expected atomic cycle means VAD silently fused two bursts together — the earliest, most sensitive fingerprint of the failure, firing before capture rate visibly drops.

Early-stop (experiments 2-3)

Once a cell shows a genuine plateau or a merge event, it runs 10 more seconds for confirmation, then moves on — instead of always burning its full 5-minute budget. One cell in experiment 3 stopped after 65s instead of 300s with an already-clear 25% capture rate.

How the finding changed — three experiments

Each step below was designed to test whether the previous step's conclusion actually held up. It didn't, twice.

Experiment 1 · 11 cells · escalate then combine

A parametric corner appears: 97% density + 2× rate + 10s length

Push density, rate, and length to their max one at a time (all pass cleanly), then combine pairs (all pass cleanly), then combine all three (35% capture, latency up 2 orders of magnitude). Looked like a clean three-way interaction effect — the natural conclusion was "this specific corner of (density, rate, length) space is where the system breaks."

Experiment 2 · 14 cells · densify the grid around that corner

The corner is real and sharp — but a new recording breaks it anyway, at milder settings

10 new density×rate combinations at the same 10s length, filling in the gaps between the known PASS/FAIL points, all came back completely clean — confirming the failure is a genuinely sharp, isolated point, not a gradual surface. Then a new recording was added mid-investigation (9s.wav) at those SAME "safe" settings — and it failed at three different combinations, including ones two steps inside the boundary that was clean for the 10s recording. A control run ruled out "9s.wav is just a bad recording": at mild settings it was perfectly clean too. The failure needed both stress AND this specific content.

Experiment 3 · 14 cells · one recording, cropped to 7 different lengths

Decisive: the same recording fails non-monotonically as length varies

To separate "duration" from "which recording" for good, one 17.152-second recording was truncated to 9 through 15 seconds (1s steps) — every length shares the identical leading audio, only the cutoff differs. At the worst combo (97%/2×), the result was fail at 9s, clean at 10-14s, fail again at 15s. A real capacity ceiling would fail once past some duration and stay failed. This didn't — it fails, recovers, and fails again, which only makes sense if the trigger is where the crop happens to land relative to this recording's own acoustic structure, not duration as a number.

Interpretation

The revised finding

There is no clean capacity ceiling in (density, rate, length) space. Extreme stress — roughly ≥95% density combined with ≥1.75× rate — is a necessary condition: nothing failed at mild settings anywhere across all 39 cells run in this investigation. But it is not sufficient. Experiment 3 proves this directly: the identical recording, cropped to different lengths at the identical worst-case density/rate, fails at 9s, passes cleanly at 10-14s, and fails again at 15s. No smooth function of duration produces that shape.

The real trigger is content-specific. Whether a given cell fails depends on where a segment's boundary happens to land relative to that audio's own trailing acoustic structure, once TSM-stretched and packed at high density. This is a rarer and more specific failure mode than "the pipeline runs out of capacity" — it's a VAD/TSM correctness interaction that only a minority of extreme-stress, specific-content combinations happen to trigger.

Two visibly different failure signatures

a genuine backlog vs. a content-triggered misdetection

The original experiment 1 FAIL corner (97%/2×/10s) shows sustained, ever-worsening latency (up to 17s) and decode-time-per-audio-second at or above 1.0× realtime — a genuine, compounding backlog. The experiment 2/3 failures (9s.wav, and the 9s/15s single-source crops) show lower decode-time-per-second than the clean cells right next to them, an oscillating (not climbing) latency pattern, and near-immediate onset (as early as t=0). These are not the same mechanism: one is capacity exhaustion, the other is VAD misjudging a specific acoustic boundary, independent of whether the system could otherwise keep up.

Why a single recording per length was the right fix

isolating the one variable that mattered

Experiments 1 and 2 each used a different real recording per length value, which meant "length" and "which sentence" were always confounded — a limitation stated explicitly at the time, not discovered later by accident. Experiment 2's 9s.wav result made that confound impossible to ignore: identical density/rate, different content, opposite outcome. Experiment 3's crop-from-one-source design was built specifically to settle it, and did — content, not duration, is the dominant variable once density and rate are already extreme.

The "split" artifact is real but not a failure

7s/10s recordings occasionally split into two segments

Every experiment-1 cell using the 7s or 10s recording shows a captured-segment count noticeably above the nominal expected count (e.g. 51 vs. 27 for the 10s-length cell). This is a benign, already-characterized pattern: a long sentence's own internal pause occasionally causes VAD to split it into a ~9-10s main body plus a ~1.2s tail, which briefly queues behind the main segment's decode. It's bounded, it recurs identically, and it doesn't compound — a quirk of that recording's content, not a system limit.

Why capture rate and merge events, not queue depth or CPU

calibrated against known failures, not assumed

Queue depth and CPU% were both checked against the original confirmed failure before experiment 1 was designed, and both missed it: queue depth stayed at 0/0/0 throughout (it's sampled only when a transcript arrives, by which point the backlog has drained), and CPU looked statistically identical to a healthy run. Merge-event detection (added for experiments 2-3) turned out to be the most sensitive signal of all — it fires on the exact mechanism (a VAD-fused segment) rather than a downstream symptom, and fires early enough to drive the early-stop optimization that made a 39-cell investigation practical on a single Pi.

A design flaw was found and fixed mid-experiment-1. The original plan controlled segment length synthetically — repeating a short clip back-to-back with zero gap so VAD would perceive one continuous longer segment. This failed empirically: VAD split the zero-gap splice back into separate segments every time, and at 1.5× rate it produced 100% garbage/repetitive decodes. The design was changed to real recordings at each target length, which reintroduces the content confound that experiments 2 and 3 ultimately had to resolve.

Reliability was disabled throughout (idle_flush_s, segment_limits_enabled, the in-process supervisor all off), to find the raw capacity/correctness boundary of the decode/VAD pipeline itself. A production deployment runs with these enabled, which gives real additional margin beyond anything reported here — this is the floor, not the deployed behavior.

Early-stop mechanics. A cell stops ~10s after the first sustained latency plateau (3+ consecutive segments with pre-STT wait >3× that segment's own decode time) or merge event (a segment-start gap >1.3× the expected atomic cycle), instead of always running the full 5-minute budget. Actual overshoot beyond that 10s can reach one more full loop of the WAV file, since the feed loop only checks between loops, not mid-playback.

Latency definition. End-to-end = time from an utterance's audio finishing ingest to its final transcript being published (VAD → STT → publish), the same definition used throughout this project's benchmarks.
